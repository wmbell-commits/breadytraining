ADVANCED & ELECTRIC EQUIPMENT

The forklift aisle is changing.

Modern fleets increasingly combine electric powertrains with electronic steering, height and position aids, laser or wire guidance, access-control systems, impact sensing, telematics, electronic pre-operation checklists and fleet-management software. These systems can improve control and accountability, but they do not replace operator competence, a required pre-use inspection, or training on the truck's actual controls, warnings and operating limitations.

B-Ready experience: Wayne has helped install and train on a high-lift turret truck. Specialized narrow-aisle/high-lift equipment deserves equipment-specific training because steering, operator position, visibility, load handling, guidance systems and emergency procedures can differ significantly from a conventional counterbalanced forklift.

OSHA requires training on the controls and instrumentation, motor operation, steering and maneuvering, visibility, inspections, operating limitations, manufacturer warnings and the specific types of truck the operator is authorized to operate. Review OSHA PIT training requirements.

Fleet monitoring & electronic prechecks

Electronic checklists and telematics can document completion, restrict truck access, identify impacts and help supervisors see trends. Treat them as tools within the safety program—not as substitutes for a meaningful inspection or competent supervision. Operators still need to understand what they are checking, recognize defects and follow the employer/manufacturer procedure when a truck should be removed from service.

Guided & highly automated trucks

Laser-guided, wire-guided and other assisted systems can change how a truck steers, positions or travels. Training should address the system actually installed, transitions between guided and manual operation where applicable, pedestrian interaction, system limitations, alarms, emergency procedures and manufacturer instructions. Fully automated/driverless vehicle systems may involve requirements beyond conventional operator training, so B-Ready evaluates the application before representing it as a standard PIT operator course.

Common topics

Pre-use inspections, stability triangle, capacity and load center, ramps and grades, pedestrians, docks and trailers, attachments, battery and propane safety, parking, visibility, narrow aisles, guidance systems and site-specific hazards.

The rules people tend to miss.

Short practical reminders for operators, trainers and safety managers. These notes are learning aids—not a substitute for the applicable OSHA/MIOSHA rule, manufacturer instructions, workplace procedures or the complete ANSI/SAIA/ITSDF standard.

PIT / Forklift safety notes

Why is it called the stability triangle?

Many counterbalanced forklifts behave as a three-point suspension system: the two front-wheel contact points form the front of the triangle and the rear axle pivots about its center, creating the third support point. Stability depends on keeping the combined center of gravity inside that triangle.

Learn the concept from OSHA's Powered Industrial Trucks eTool below.

Training is workplace-specific.

Forklift qualification is more than a classroom card. OSHA's PIT rule requires formal instruction, practical training and evaluation, including workplace-related topics that apply to the operator's actual environment.

AWP / MEWP safety notes

Can you exit an elevated platform?

Do not teach this as a blanket “yes.” Exiting or entering an elevated MEWP is a special-use situation that must be addressed through the applicable standard, manufacturer instructions, employer risk assessment and site procedure. The normal expectation is to remain within the platform and use the access system as intended.

Pre-use means functional checks too.

A pre-use inspection is not just a walk-around. Operators should verify applicable controls, emergency functions and safety devices in accordance with the manufacturer and employer procedure before operation. If an upper-limit or other limiting device is part of the required functional check for that machine, verify it before use.

Know the rescue plan before going up.

Operators and ground personnel should know how emergency lowering works and what to do if the platform operator cannot lower the machine normally.

Machine type matters.

Scissor lifts and boom-supported MEWPs do not present identical hazards. Fall protection, stability, positioning, slope limits and operating procedures must match the equipment and manufacturer instructions.

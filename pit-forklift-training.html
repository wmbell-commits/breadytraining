ADVANCED & ELECTRIC EQUIPMENT

The forklift aisle is changing.

Modern fleets increasingly combine electric powertrains with electronic steering, height and position aids, laser or wire guidance, access-control systems, impact sensing, telematics, electronic pre-operation checklists and fleet-management software. These systems can improve control and accountability, but they do not replace operator competence, required inspections or training on the truck's actual controls, warnings and operating limitations.

B-Ready experience: Wayne has helped install and train on a high-lift turret truck. Specialized narrow-aisle/high-lift equipment deserves equipment-specific training because steering, operator position, visibility, load handling, guidance systems and emergency procedures can differ significantly from a conventional counterbalanced forklift.

Fleet monitoring & electronic prechecks

Electronic checklists and telematics can document completion, restrict truck access, identify impacts and help supervisors see trends. Treat them as tools within the safety program—not substitutes for a meaningful inspection or competent supervision.

Guided & highly automated trucks

Laser-guided, wire-guided and other assisted systems can change how a truck steers, positions or travels. Training should address the system actually installed, transitions between guided and manual operation where applicable, pedestrian interaction, limits, alarms, emergency procedures and manufacturer instructions.
